1 ADMISSION
Admission to the Universityshall be open to all persons irrespective of caste, class,
creed, gender, abilities, or nationality. All admissions shall be made on the basis
of merit in the qualifying examinations and as per the rules and guidelines
prescribed by the Government of Karnataka, other concerned regulatory bodies
and the entrance examinations conducted by the University.
The students shall be admitted to a Program subject to fulfilment of eligibility
criteria, as prescribed by the University for the respective Program.
A student admitted to a Program of study, shall continue to remain registered for
such a Program, till they successfully complete the Program or withdraw from the
Program in accordance with the concerned Program Regulations.
Every student duly admitted to the University for a Program after compliance with
prescribed formalities and payment of prescribed fees as applicable and other
amounts and submission of prescribed documents and certificates shall be allotted
a University Enrolment Number OR UEN.

examined for eligibility for admission as per the procedure and criteria laid down
in the Academic Regulations of the University and the concerned Program
Regulations, and admission shall be dependent on their eligibility so determined.

of the eligibility criteria.

activities. The University is particularly committed to enabling meritorious students with
financial constraints, differently abled students and achievers at the state and national
levels, through appropriate scholarships.

5.1.2 Outstanding achievement in State / National / International Level Entrance

### 1. Academic Calendar

1.1. The academic activities of the University are regulated by the Academic
Calendar approved by the Academic Council and notified by the
University at least one calendar month before the commencement of each
academic year.
1.2. An academic year in the University shall normally be divided into two
semesters consisting of ninety (90) University working days each, known
as ‘Odd Semester’ (normally from July / August to December) and ‘Even
Semester’ (normally from January to April/May).
1.3. An additional Academic Term known as the ‘Summer Term’ may be
scheduled during the summer break (June to July).

### 3. Academic Credits - Course Credit Structure

The Credit Structure is used to define various types of Courses to provide for
the appropriate pedagogy and methods of assessment and evaluation. The
flexibility required to accomplish the Course learning objectives and outcomes
can be provided for, while retaining a common framework for Credit allocation.
More importantly, it is necessary to have a transparent, credible and robust
system for planning, delivery and evaluation of each Course of the diverse
Programs of study of the University.
3.1. The Credit Structure for defining and categorizing Courses is the L – T –
P (Lecture – Tutorial – Practical) framework. The Course Credits are fixed
based on the following norms:
Lecture / Tutorial: One (01) contact/classroom hour per week is
assigned One (01) Credit.
Practical: Two (02) hours per week of practice/studio/laboratory/field
and other similar practice or skill development components, are assigned
One (01) Credit.
For example:
A Course with L – T – P structure of 4 – 0 – 0 will be assigned 4 Credits.
A Course with L – T – P structure of 3 – 1 – 0 will be assigned 4 Credits.
A Course with L – T – P structure of 0 – 0 – 4 will be assigned 2 Credits.
A Course with L – T – P structure of 1 – 0 – 4 will be assigned 3 Credits.
3.2. Practice/Skill based Courses like Industry Internship, Capstone,
Research Dissertation, Integrative Studio, Interdisciplinary Project and
such similar Courses, and Non -Taught Credit Courses like Seminar,
Summer / Short Internship, Social Engagement / Field Projects and
such similar Courses, where the pedagogy does not lend itself to a typical
L-T-P structure as defined in Clause 3.1, are assigned the number of
Credits based on the quantum of work/effort required to fulfil the
learning objectives and outcomes prescribed for the concerned Courses.
<!-- page:20 -->
3.3. A student earns credits by satisfactorily completing Courses and other
academic activities every Academic Term. The credits associated with a
Course are dependent upon the number of hours of instruction per week
in that Course. Similarly, the credit associated with any of the other
learning activities is dependent upon the quantum of work expected to
be put in for each of the other learning activities. In general, a certain
quantum of academic work measured in terms of Credits is laid down as
the minimum requirements for the award of a particular Degree.

### 7. Attendance Requirements

7.1. In order to maintain academic rigour and excellence, all students must
attend every lecture, tutorial, studio, field work, laboratory, practical
classes and all other such curricular sessions as prescribed by the Program
requirements.
7.2. To account for approved leave of absence to appear for end semester
examination, the attendance requirement shall be a minimum of seventy
five percent (75%) of the classes actually conducted in every Course the
student has registered for in the Academic Term.
7.3. Further, if a student or their family member/s suffer serious medical
exigencies of hospitalization, trauma or contagious disease, the concerned
student may submit a leave request , together with documents to the
Faculty Advisor [Mentor] for recommendation to the Program Chair. The
student shall be given an additional relaxation in attendance in the
Course(s) where there is a shortage subject to the approval by the Vice -
Chancellor. However, on no account whatsoever, shall the minimum
requirement of attendance be less than sixty five percent (65% ) of the
classes actually conducted in every Course the student has registered for
in the Academic Term. The student shall not be eligible for this special
provision if they fail to produce authentic medical certificates and relevant
documents, including fitness certificate (in the case of student only), on the
day of rejoining the classes, in support of the medical exigency.
7.4. If a student has been selected to represent the State and/or India in State
/ National / International Events / Competitions, the concerned student
may be given relaxation in attendance requirement (in the Course(s) where
there is a shortage) for the concerned period of absence recommended by
the Director, Campus Life and the Faculty Advisor [Mentor] to the Program
Chair and thereafter approval by the Vice Chancellor . However, on no
account whatsoever, shall the minimum requirement of attendance be less
than sixty five percent (65%) of the classes actually conducted in every
Course the student has registered for in the Academic Term. The student
shall not be eligible for this special provision if they fail to produce
authentic certificates of participation in the aforementioned Events /
Completions.

7.5. Further, where attendance requirements are prescribed by Government
Regulatory Bodies for specific Programs, the same shall also be mandatorily
adhered to without exception. Such specific requirements, as applicable,
shall be prescribed in the Program Regulations of the concerned Program
of study.
7.6. Shortage of Attendance:
A student with shortage of attendance (as specified in Clause 7.2 and the
applicable provisions of Clauses 7.3, 7.4 and 7.5) shall not be permitted to
appear for the End Term Examinations, as applicable, of the Course(s) in
which the attendance shortfall exists, irrespective of the student’s academic
performance in the final components of Continuous Assessments. The
student shall be declared as having failed to complete the Course due to
shortage of attendance and shall be given the ‘FA’ Grade (Refer to Sub -
Clause 8.13.3) in the concerned Course(s).
The student is cautioned that shortage of attendance may result in the loss
of an Academic Year. It is the sole responsibility of the student to ensure
that they do not fall under shortage of attendance.

8.12. Cut-off Marks for ‘O’ and ‘F’ Grades
The cut-off marks for ‘O’ grade and for ‘F’ grade (aggregate of the marks

8.13. Description of Grades:
8.13.1 ‘O’ Grade
An ‘O’ grade stands for outstanding achievement, relative to the
class, and the Course Instructor shall take utmost care in awarding
this highest grade.
8.13.2 ‘D’ Grade
The ‘D’ grade stands for marginal performance and is the minimum
passing grade.
8.13.3 ‘F’ and ‘FA’ Grades
a) The ‘F’ grade denotes very poor performance, i.e., failure in a Course
due to poor performance and the Course Instructor shall take utmost
care while awarding this lowest grade.
Further, ‘F’ grade shall be given to a student who is Absent for the
End Term Examination as specified for a concerned Course. If the
<!-- page:26 -->
absence is solely due to medical exigencies specifically
hospitalization, trauma or contagious disease only, the placeholder
Grade ‘I’ will be given (Refer Sub Clause 8.13.5).
b) ‘FA’ grade denotes poor attendance i.e., failure in a Course due to
attendance shortage (Refer Clause 7.6).
c) The students who have received ‘F’ grade in a Course in the
immediate subsequent semester may be allowed to appear for a
Make-Up Examination. The Make -Up Examinations will be
conducted possibly along with that arranged for those students
who received the ‘I’ grade, within the period announced in the
Academic Calendar.
d) Only regular registrants of a given Course during a given Academic
Semester who have obtained ‘F’ or ‘I’ grade in the Course will be
permitted to appear for the Make-up Examination.
e) Students who continue to have ‘F’ grade after the Make -Up
Examination are required to re-register for the Course whenever it
is offered subsequently.
f) All the ‘F’ and ‘FA’ grades secured in any Course shall be recorded
on the grade card.
g) A student who obtains ‘FA’ grade in any Course shall re-register for
the Course in the subsequent Academic Term whenever the Course
is offered until a passing grade is obtained. However, for a course
that is not mandatory in which ‘FA’ grade has been obtained, the
student may either repeat the same Course or register for any other
Course (from the list of approved Courses). The student is
permitted to opt for change of Minor only once till completion of the
program.
8.13.4 ‘S’ and ‘U’ Grades
a) Grades ‘S’ and ‘U’ do not have any associated grade points and
are recommended for use in the evaluation of select Non -Taught
Credit Courses [ Summer Internship ], as applicable, and for
grading Audit Courses.
b) The ‘S’ grade denotes satisfactory performance and completion of
a Course. The associated credits are earned and counted for the
mandatory credits requirements as specified in the concerned
Program Curriculum.
c) The ‘U’ grade denotes unsatisfactory performance and non -
completion of a Course. The student shall have to re -register for
<!-- page:27 -->
the concerned Course, if mandatory, until the ‘S’ grade is
awarded. This clause is also applicable to Audit Courses.
d) In the event that the student secures a U grade in an audit course,
which is non mandatory it will be deemed that such a student has
discontinued the course. The U grade will not be reflected in the
transcript.
e) The ‘S’ or ‘U’ grade secured in a Course shall be recorded on the
Grade Card.
8.13.5 ‘I’ Grade
‘I’ is a placeholder grade which denotes “incomplete” performance in
any Course or Courses, due to one of the following conditions:
a) Absence at the End Term Examination solely due to medical
exigencies, specifically hospitalization, trauma or contagious
disease. The ‘I’ grade shall be replaced by an appropriate letter
grade after the student clears the Make -Up Examination (Refer
Clause 9.4).
b) In case of a reported case of unfair means (plagiarism,
malpractice, etc.) under investigation against a student in the End
Term Examination or any component of assessment of any
Course(s), the student will be given an I Grade . The placeholder
grade ‘I’ shall be replaced with a regular grade based on
recommendations of the panel constituted to investigate the case,
and the subsequent approval of the Registrar. Students will not
be eligible for the Make-up examination if the investigating panel
recommends a ‘F’ grade.
The Course(s) in which a student has received ‘I’ grade shall not
be included in the SGPA/CGPA calculations (Refer Clause 8.15).

8.15 Transcript:
A University Transcript is an official academic document that comprehensively
records a student's academic progress throughout their time at the university.
It also serves as a formal reference for determining eligibility to receive the degree
certificate during convocation. The transcript provides detailed information on
every course a student has registered for and successfully completed, including
the Course Code, Course Title, Grade Obtained, Credit, Semester of
Completion, and Course Bucket (e.g., University Core, Program Foundation,
etc.).
The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is computed based solely on
courses that carry grade points. However, the following rules apply:
x Mandatory courses graded as S (Satisfactory) or U (Unsatisfactory) are
listed in the transcript but are excluded from CGPA calculation, as they do
not carry grade points.
x Audit courses are included only if the student earns an 'S' grade. If a 'U'
grade is received in an audit course, it is omitted from the transcript.
x If a student fails to complete a course in the original semester but later
completes it through re-registration, only the most recent valid grade is
recorded.
x Courses from which a student hasformally withdrawn do not appear in the
transcript.
x In case a student opts to exit before completion of the degree program, the
transcript will be issued upon request with all grades obtained by the
student at that point of time.
In addition, the transcript outlines the mandatory credit requirements
fulfilled by the student across key academic categories such as University
Core, Program Foundation, Program Core, Specialization, and Minor.

9.1. Continuous Assessments includes components such as Quiz,
Presentations, Take Home Assignments, Group Discussions etc., which

9.5. Make-Up Examinations
Make-Up Examinations are a provision for a student to complete a Course
(or Courses) in which she/he received an ‘F’ grade or was given the
placeholder grade ‘I’ to reappear in the End Term Examination component
of a Course (or Courses), subject to the conditions mentioned below in
Sub-Clauses 9.5.1 and 9.5.2. Students may also avail the benefit of Make-
up examination to improve her/his CGPA. In no other circumstances shall
Make-Up Examinations be available to students.
9.5.1 A student who fails to appear for the End Term Examinations
in some or all Courses due to medical exigencies, specifically
hospitalization, trauma or contagious disease and the said student
informs the Program Chair/Dean concerned in a timely manner (i.e.,
on or before thecommencement of the said End Term Examinations),
may submit a request to the Registrar [Evaluation] of the University
within the duly notified dates, along with the prescribed fee for the
Make-Up Examinations fixed by the University from time to time. On
the basis of the student’s performance in the Make-Up Examinations
and considering the marks obtained by the student in all other
<!-- page:31 -->
Continuous Assessments, as applicable, the final letter grade
awarded shall replace the placeholder grade ‘I’.
In case, the application of the student for Make -Up Examinations is
rejected by the Registrar [Evaluation], the student shall be declared
“Failed” in the concerned Course(s) and the placeholder grade ‘I’ shall
be replaced with ‘F’ grade in the concerned Course(s). Further, the
student shall have to complete the Course(s) as per the provisions
and conditions prescribed in Clause 11 and Sub-Clause 11.4.2.
Further, if the concerned student does not avail the Make -Up
Examinations, or is absent for the Make -Up Examinations, the
student shall be declared “Failed” in the concerned Course(s) and the
placeholder grade ‘I’ shall be replaced with ‘F’ grade. Further, the
student shall have to complete the Course(s) as per the provisions
and conditions prescribed in Clause 11 and Sub-Clause 11.4.2.
9.5.2 Any student may avail the benefit of Make -up examination to
improve her/his CGPA or to enable her/him to satisfy the
progression criteria as prescribed in Section 12. The student shall
submit the registration form for the Make -Up Examinations for
approval to the Registrar (Evaluation) of the University within the
duly notified date, along with the prescribed fee for the Make -Up
Examinations fixed by the University from time to time.
Further, if a student registers for the Make -up examination, the
existing grades obtained in the concerned Course(s) shall be declared
null and avoid, unless the student secures a lower grade in the Make-

receives in the concerned Course(s).

for Practice / Skill based Courses based and Non -Taught Credit

Grade in such a Course, the student shall complete the concerned

### 10. Academic Appeals

10.1. The University is committed to keep the entire process of evaluation
transparent. Within three (03) working days of announcement of results,
if a student needs clarification on the marks / grade obtained, the Course
instructor shall explain to a student why they received the grade awarded.
A mechanism for review of grades is incorporated in the evaluation
system.
10.2. The student may appeal for review of grades to the AAB [Academic
Appeal Board] before the date specified in the Academic Calendar or
notification issued by the University to this effect.
10.3. The fee for such an appeal shall be notified by the University from time
to time.
10.4. The AAB shall submit its report and recommendation to the Vice
Chancellor within five (05) University working days from the date of
appeal. The approval and decision of the Vice Chancellor shall be final and
binding on all concerned. The decision of the Vice Chancellor, to change
or retain the awarded grade, shall be duly notified and effected accordingly
by the Registrar (Evaluation).

## Section VI - Prevention of Sexual Harassment

<!-- page:57 -->
Guidelines on Prevention of Sexual Harassment
1. Introduction
The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal)
Act, 2013 ("POSH Act") was enacted as a comprehensive legislation to provide a safe, secure
and enabling environment, free from sexual harassment to every woman. This statute was
enacted to fill the legislative void which had been partially addressed by the judiciary in
Vishaka and Others v. State of Rajasthan and Others (1997 (7) SCC 323). In this seminal
public interest litigation verdict, the Supreme Court of India had framed a set of guidelines
("Vishaka Guidelines") for dealing with instances of sexual harassment at the workplace,
which has now been codified in the POSH Act.
The Supreme Court of India held that sexual harassment of women is a violation of the
fundamental right of women to work in a safe environment.
The Supreme Court has defined ‘sexual harassment’ to include:
1.1 "An unwanted conduct with sexual undertones if it occurs or which is persistent and
which demeans, humiliates or creates a hostile and intimidating environment or is
calculated to induce submission by actual or threatened adverse consequences and
includes any one or more or all of the following unwelcome acts or behavior (whether
directly or by implication), namely; -
a) any unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of sexual nature;
b) demand or request for sexual favours;
c) making sexually colored remarks
d) physical contact and advances; or
e) showing pornography
1.2 any one (or more than one or all) of the following circumstances, if it occurs or is
present in relation or connected with any behavior that has explicit or implicit sexual
undertones-
a) implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment as quid pro quo for sexual
favors;
b) implied or explicit threat of detrimental treatment in the conduct of work;
c) implied or explicit threat about the present or future status of the person
concerned;
d) creating an intimidating offensive or hostile learning environment;
<!-- page:58 -->
e) humiliating treatment likely to affect the health, safety dignity or physical
integrity of the person concerned;
As a follow-up to the POSH Act, the University Grants Commission (UGC) – the apex
body for all higher education institutions - has come up with a regulation titled
University Grants Commission (Prevention, prohibition and redressal of sexual
harassment of women employees and students in higher educational institutions)
Regulations, 2015. These regulations apply to all higher education institutions in
India. The provisions of the regulation are being adhered to by Vidyashilp University.
2. Responsibility of the University
VU commits to a zero -tolerance policy against sexual harassment. The management
reinforces that it will create a campus free from discrimination, harassment, retaliation or
sexual assault at all levels. VU will ensure compliance with all provisions of the regulation
and make the following efforts toward the same:
a) Constitute an Internal Complaints Committee to proactively curb all forms of
harassment of employees and students.
b) Publicly notify the provisions against sexual harassment and ensure their wide
dissemination in the prospectus, notice boards as well as the website, the penalty and
consequences of sexual harassment.
c) Organise training programmes/ workshops for the officers, functionaries , faculty and
students, as indicated in the SAKSHAM Report (Measures for Ensuring the Safety of
Women and Programmes for Gender Sensitization on Campuses) of the UGC, to
sensitize them and ensure knowledge and awareness of the rights, entitlements and
responsibilities enshrined in the Act and under these regulations. These include
sessions to create awareness about what constitutes sexual harassment including
hostile environment harassment and quid pro quo harassment
d) Act decisively against all gender -based violence perpetrated against employees and
students of all sexes.
e) Bring to book those guilty of sexual harassment against its employees and students.
f) Monitor the timely submission of reports by the ICC as well as prepare an annual status
report with details on the number of cases filed and their disposal and submit the same
to the Commission.
3. Constitution of ICC
VU has established an Internal Complaints Committee to prevent, prohibit and redress and
issues of sexual harassment. The ICC constitutes a lady presiding officer who is a senior
teaching faculty member, 2 other teaching faculty members, 2 members from th e non-
<!-- page:59 -->
teaching, 3 students and an external member who works for an association that is
committed to the cause of women.
4. Role of ICC
a) provide assistance if an employee or a student chooses to file a complaint with the
police;
b) provide mechanisms or dispute redressal and dialogue to address issues through a
just and fair conciliation without undermining the complainant's rights, and minimize
the need for purely punitive approaches that lead to further resentment, alienation or
violence ;
c) protect the safety of the complainant by not divulging the person's identity, and provide
the mandatory relief by way of sanctioned leave or relaxation or attendance requirement
or transfer to another department or supervisor as required during the pendency of the
complaint, or also provide for the transfer of the offender;
d) ensure that victims or witnesses are not victimized or discriminated against while
dealing with complaints of sexual harassment; and
e) ensure the prohibition of retaliation or adverse action against a covered individual
because the employee or the student is engaged in protected activity.
5. How to lodge a complaint?
An aggrieved person is required to submit a written complaint to the ICC within three
months from the date of the incident and in case of a series of incidents within a period of
three months from the date of the last incident.
The Presiding Officer or any Member of the ICC shall render all reasonable assistance to
the person for making the complaint in writing.
6. Process and timeline of conducting an inquiry
a) The ICC shall, upon receipt of the complaint, send one copy of the complaint to the
respondent within a period of seven days of such receipt.
b) Upon receipt of the copy of the complaint, the respondent shall file his or her reply to
the complaint along with the list of documents, and names and addresses of witnesses
within a period of ten days.
c) The inquiry has to be completed within a period of ninety days from the receipt of the
complaint. The inquiry report, with recommendations, if any, has to be submitted
within ten days from the completion of the inquiry to the Vice Chancellor of VU. Copy
of the findings or recommendations shall also be served on both parties to the
complaint.
<!-- page:60 -->
d) The Vice Chancellor shall act on the recommendations of the committee within a period
of thirty days from the receipt of the inquiry report unless an appeal against the findings
is filed within that time by either party.
e) An appeal against the findings or /recommendations of the ICC may be filed by either
party before the Vice Chancellor within a period of thirty days from the date of the
recommendations.
f) The aggrieved party may seek conciliation in order to settle the matter. No monetary
settlement should be made as a basis of conciliation. The conciliation process shall be
facilitated through the ICC
g) The identities of the aggrieved party or victim or the witness or the offender shall not be
made public or kept in the public domain, especially during the process of the inquiry.
7. Action against offenders
a) Anyone found guilty of sexual harassment shall be punished in accordance with the
service rules of VU if the offender is an employee.
b) Where the respondent is a student, depending upon the severity of the offence VU may
i) withhold privileges of the student such as access to the library, auditoria, halls of
residence, transportation, scholarships, allowances, and identity card;
ii) suspend or restrict entry into the campus for a specific period;
iii) expel and strike off the name from the rolls of VU, including denial of readmission,
if the offence so warrants;
iv) award reformative punishments like mandatory counselling and, or, the
performance of community services.
8. Action Against False or frivolous Complaints
To ensure that the provisions for the protection of employees and students from sexual
harassment do not get misused, provisions against false or malicious complaints have to be
made. If the ICC concludes that the allegations made were false, malicious or the complaint
was made knowing it to be untrue, or forged or misleading information has been provided
during the inquiry, the complainant shall be liable to be punished.

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal)
Act, 2013 ("POSH Act") was enacted as a comprehensive legislation to provide a safe, secure
and enabling environment, free from sexual harassment to every woman. This statute was
enacted to fill the legislative void which had been partially addressed by the judiciary in
Vishaka and Others v. State of Rajasthan and Others (1997 (7) SCC 323). In this seminal
public interest litigation verdict, the Supreme Court of India had framed a set of guidelines
("Vishaka Guidelines") for dealing with instances of sexual harassment at the workplace,
which has now been codified in the POSH Act.
The Supreme Court of India held that sexual harassment of women is a violation of the
fundamental right of women to work in a safe environment.

directly or by implication), namely; -
